How the 10-year rule works

For most non-spouse beneficiaries of an owner who died in 2020 or later, the inherited IRA must be emptied by December 31 of the tenth year after the year of death. Whether annual RMDs are also required in years 1 to 9 depends on when the owner died.

Owner diedYears 1 to 9Year 10
Before their required beginning dateNo annual RMDs requiredEmpty the account
On or after their required beginning dateAnnual RMDs required (from 2025 on)Empty the account

Final IRS regulations require the annual RMDs starting in 2025, and the IRS waived penalties for missed annual RMDs from 2021 through 2024. The 10-year clock still started in the year after death, so those waived years do not extend the deadline.

How the annual RMD is figured

Divide the prior year-end balance by a life expectancy from the IRS Single Life Table (Publication 590-B, Table I). Look up the beneficiary's age in the year after death, then subtract one for each later year. When the owner died after their required beginning date, the beneficiary can use the owner's remaining life expectancy instead if it is longer; the calculator uses whichever is longer.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Eligible designated beneficiaries who stretch over their life expectancy, and spouses who treat the IRA as their own.
  • Trusts, estates and charities as beneficiaries, and multiple beneficiaries sharing one account.
  • Inherited Roth IRAs (no annual RMDs, but still the 10-year deadline) and any RMD the owner had not yet taken in the year of death.
