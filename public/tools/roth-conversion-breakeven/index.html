How the breakeven is calculated

The calculator compares two paths for the same dollars. Without a conversion, the money grows in the traditional IRA and is taxed at the future rate when withdrawn, while the cash that would have paid the conversion tax stays invested in a taxable account. With a conversion, that cash pays the tax today and the full amount grows tax-free in the Roth. The breakeven is the first year the Roth path is worth more after tax.

What drives the answer

  • Today's rate versus the future rate. This matters most. If the future rate is equal or higher, converting comes out ahead almost immediately.
  • Where the tax comes from. Paying from outside savings moves more money into the Roth. Paying from the IRA shrinks the conversion, and before age 59½ the withheld portion can face a 10% penalty.
  • Time and growth. When today's rate is higher, a long horizon and tax drag on outside savings can still make a conversion pay off.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Medicare IRMAA surcharges, which a conversion can trigger two years later.
  • The Roth five-year rules and early-withdrawal penalties.
  • Lower future RMDs, Social Security taxation and state residency changes.
  • Converting across several years to fill a tax bracket. Run each year's amount separately.
