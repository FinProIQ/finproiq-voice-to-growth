How the RMD is calculated

Divide the account balance on December 31 of the prior year by the distribution period for the age the client reaches this year. The distribution period comes from the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table (Publication 590-B, Appendix B, Table III), in effect since 2022. At 73 it is 26.5, so a $500,000 balance requires $18,868.

When RMDs start

BornRMD age
1950 or earlierAlready taking RMDs (started at 70½ or 72)
1951 to 195973
1960 or later75

The first RMD can wait until April 1 of the following year. Every later RMD is due by December 31. Delaying the first one means two RMDs in the same tax year. Final IRS regulations reserve the 1959 birth year; this calculator uses 73, as proposed regulations do.

What this calculator does not cover

  • A spouse more than 10 years younger who is the sole beneficiary (uses the Joint Life Table, which gives a smaller RMD).
  • Inherited IRAs and the 10-year rule.
  • 401(k) and 403(b) plans, where the still-working exception may delay RMDs. Each plan's RMD must come from that plan.
  • Roth IRAs, which have no RMDs for the original owner.
