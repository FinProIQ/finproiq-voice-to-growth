How the benefit changes with claiming age

Claiming before full retirement age reduces the benefit by 5/9 of 1% for each of the first 36 months early and 5/12 of 1% for each month beyond that. Waiting past full retirement age adds delayed retirement credits of 2/3 of 1% a month (8% a year) up to age 70, for anyone born in 1943 or later.

Reading the breakeven age

The breakeven age is when the larger, later benefit has paid out as much in total as the smaller, earlier one. A client expected to live past it collects more by waiting. Cost-of-living adjustments raise every option by the same percentage, so they do not move the breakeven.

What this calculator does not cover

  • Spousal and survivor benefits. For married clients, the higher earner's claiming age also sets the survivor benefit.
  • The earnings test, which withholds benefits for clients who claim before full retirement age and keep working.
  • Taxation of benefits, investment returns on early benefits, and claiming in months between birthdays.
